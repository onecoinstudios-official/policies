Privacy Policy

Effective 5 November 2026 · Version 1.2

Bento Rush is a 60-second match-3 game. This policy explains what the game, and the three Google services it uses, do with information — in plain terms and without small print.

No account, no sign-in, no profile. Your scores stay on your device. Ads are contextual and family-safe — never based on who you are.

What stays on your device

Your scores and statistics (best score, longest streak, biggest cascade, tiles cleared, per-ingredient totals, accuracy) are stored on your device only, using the device’s standard local key-value storage. This data:

  • never leaves your device;
  • is not backed up to our servers (we have none);
  • disappears if you uninstall the Game or clear its storage from your operating system’s app settings.

What we collect

Bento Rush itself keeps your scores locally. Three Google services receive the following, and nothing else:

What we never collect: your name, email address, phone number, contacts, photos, precise location, advertising ID, or any account identifier. There is no sign-in, no profile, and no tracking across other apps or websites.

Advertising

Bento Rush shows a full-screen ad between rounds — at most one every few rounds, and only when you tap to start the next round. Ads come from Google AdMob.

They are contextual: chosen from what the app is, not from a profile of you. Specifically:

  • every ad request is tagged for child age treatment, which disables personalised and interest-based advertising;
  • ad content is capped at the “G” rating, suitable for all audiences;
  • the advertising-ID permission is removed from the app, so no advertising identifier exists for Google or for us to use;
  • no remarketing, no audience lists, no cross-app tracking.

Google may record that an ad was requested, shown or tapped, and may use the approximate location above to pick a suitable ad and to report aggregate statistics to us.

Analytics

We use Google Analytics for Firebase to answer questions like “do people finish their first round?” — with counts and buckets, not profiles. Four event types are sent: a round started, a round ended (score and move buckets plus cascade and streak counts), the records screen opened, and whether ads were allowed on that launch.

We never set a user ID, never attach your local scores, and never enable ad-personalisation signals. Collection stays off until the consent step below is resolved.

Crash reports

If Bento Rush ever closes unexpectedly, we want to know why. The game uses Firebase Crashlytics, Google’s crash-reporting service — the only stability and crash-monitoring tool in the game.

When something goes wrong, Crashlytics automatically sends a technical report the next time the game starts. That report contains:

  • device hardware details, model and operating-system version;
  • the app version, the screen that was open, and how far into a round the game was;
  • app state at the moment of failure, such as memory pressure and available storage;
  • the stack trace showing where the code failed.

It does not contain your name, email address, contacts, advertising ID, or precise location — and it does not record your gameplay: no move history, no tile choices, no scores. A crash report describes the state of the app, not the person playing it.

We use these reports for one purpose only: to identify, reproduce and fix bugs, crashes and performance problems. They are never used for advertising, marketing, user profiling or commercial tracking, and we do not build profiles from them. Where data-protection law requires a basis for this processing, we rely on our legitimate interest in keeping the game stable and secure.

Crash reports are processed by Google on our behalf through Firebase and Google Cloud infrastructure, retained for up to 90 days while we investigate, and then deleted. We never sell them, and we never share them with anyone for their own purposes.

If you think a report accidentally captured something personal, write to us at the address below and we will delete it.

Children

Bento Rush is made for a general audience and is suitable for children. We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, of any age.

For younger players we rely on the “support for internal operations” principle in children’s privacy law: contextual ads, aggregate analytics and crash diagnostics only — the limited data a family-safe ad, a usage count and a crash report need. There are no behavioural profiles, no persistent advertising identifiers, no personalised ads, no chat, no user-generated content, and no links out of the game other than this policy.

Parents and guardians: if you believe a child’s information has reached us, write to the address below and we will delete it.

Your choices

  • Consent. Where the law requires it — for example in the EEA and the UK — a Google-certified consent form appears before any ad loads. You can change that choice at any time from Lunch Records → Privacy options inside the game.
  • Reset the identifier. Reinstalling the game creates a new random app instance identifier.
  • Delete local records. Uninstall the game or clear its storage in your device settings.
  • Sound. Toggle audio from the speaker button on the game screen.

An optional one-time purchase can turn the between-round ads off. The purchase is billed and refunded entirely by Google Play (Google Play Billing); the game itself stores only a local "ads are off" flag. Nothing above changes because of it — no account, no receipts on any server of ours (we run none), and no new identifiers. Where the purchase is unavailable, every rule above still applies to every ad shown.

How long it is kept

Google Analytics retains event data for up to 14 months; ad-serving logs are kept for the periods described in Google’s own privacy policy, and crash reports for up to 90 days. Local scores remain until you uninstall the game or clear its storage. We run no server of our own and hold no copy of your data.

Where your data goes

Google processes this data on servers outside your country, including in the United States, under the transfer safeguards described in Google’s privacy policy. All traffic is encrypted in transit using HTTPS.

Changes

If a future version changes what is collected, this page and its effective date are updated before that version ships. Earlier versions stay in the archive so you can always see what applied when.
